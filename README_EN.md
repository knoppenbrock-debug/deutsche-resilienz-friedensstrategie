# German Resilience and Peace Strategy — English Summary

**Discussion paper 2026 · Red-Team version 0.3 · 4 October 2026**

> **Language note:** This is a Germany-focused policy model. The authoritative full version is written in German. This English summary is provided for international discussion, searchability and machine readability.  
> **[Read the authoritative German version](README.md)**

## Purpose

Germany's security, foreign, energy, economic, migration, social and infrastructure policies interact with one another. This paper therefore tests them as one system rather than as isolated policy fields.

The proposed strategic combination is:

> **Defence capability + active German peace diplomacy within Europe + conditional economic incentives + energy diversification + faster labour-market integration + prioritisation of infrastructure and a sustainable welfare state.**

Its central principle is **verification instead of trust**. Economic normalisation would follow verified de-escalation; it would not be granted in advance.

This is a policy hypothesis, not a party platform and not a claim about the motives of political actors.

## Five pillars

### A — Conditional European peace diplomacy

Europe should complement military deterrence with a permanent and operational negotiating channel. Any viable process must include Ukraine and account for the European and transatlantic actors that are decisive for security.

A possible step-by-step mechanism is:

1. a verifiable ceasefire or de-escalation measure;
2. independent verification;
3. a previously defined and limited reciprocal measure;
4. the next negotiation stage;
5. automatic reversal in the event of a serious breach.

Possible reciprocal measures could include selected sanctions relief, trade, investment or — at a later stage — energy relations, subject to EU law and common European decisions.

#### Germany's role: convene, mediate, represent interests

Germany should take a more active foreign-policy role rather than waiting for other powers to shape the diplomatic settlement of a European war. It should work inside the European Union to build a coherent and actionable common position, bring the belligerents and the actors required for a durable security settlement to the negotiating table, and help organise a continuous European negotiating process.

Germany cannot unilaterally claim to speak "for Europe". The model therefore envisages first building a robust political framework inside the EU and, where required, a common mandate. On that basis Germany could assume a leading mediating role externally — not as a supposedly neutral actor without interests, but as a European state that openly represents its legitimate security, economic and stability interests while seeking a sustainable accommodation of interests.

Success would not mean Germany single-handedly "making peace" or imposing an outcome on the parties. It would mean **creating diplomatic capacity for action**: keeping channels open, clarifying interests and red lines, negotiating verifiable intermediate steps, and working with partners to create conditions under which the belligerents can agree to a durable end to the fighting.

> **Defence capability remains a means. Diplomacy and the capacity for peace are the goal.**

Credible defence and deterrence remain necessary, but their purpose is to protect political room for manoeuvre. The strategic objective is not permanent war-readiness as a political identity, but **peace capability**: the capacity to protect interests, limit escalation, convene adversaries and help organise durable political settlements.

### B — Energy: option rather than dependence

The model does **not** propose returning to Germany's pre-2022 dependence on Russian energy. LNG capacity, Norway, storage, renewables and other suppliers remain part of the security architecture.

Under current EU law, Germany cannot unilaterally reactivate Russian gas imports as a peace incentive. A future Russian supply option would require verified de-escalation, a new European legal and political decision, competitive economics and safeguards against renewed dependence.

The German paper includes transparent sensitivity calculations. They are scenarios, **not forecasts** and not equivalent to federal-budget savings.

### C — Ukraine support: no automatic "peace dividend"

The model assumes **no saving from military support while the war continues**.

Only after a robust peace and security arrangement could parts of current expenditure potentially become available for other purposes. Even then, security guarantees, reconstruction or new defence requirements could absorb part or all of that capacity.

A counter-scenario is explicitly included: insufficient support that contributes to a worse security outcome could impose costs far larger than any short-term fiscal saving.

### D — Migration and basic income support: employment as the fiscal lever

The relevant policy target is not origin but the number of working-age people who move sustainably into employment that reduces or ends benefit dependency.

The proposed levers include earlier labour-market access, language acquisition, faster recognition of useful qualifications, childcare as employment infrastructure, effective job placement, participation requirements, rule-of-law application of residence law and measurement of sustainable employment and net fiscal effects.

The paper deliberately separates illustrative gross transfer calculations from genuine budget forecasts.

### E — Welfare state and infrastructure

Ending the war in Ukraine would not solve Germany's pension, care or infrastructure challenges. Structural reforms remain necessary to strengthen employment, productivity, implementation capacity and intergenerational sustainability.

Likewise, additional infrastructure funding alone is insufficient when planning, procurement, regulation and staffing constrain implementation.

## What the model explicitly does not claim

The paper does not claim that Ukraine aid causes Germany's welfare-state problems, that migration is their principal cause, that Russian gas would necessarily be cheap in the future, that current German energy prices are solely the result of missing Russian supply, or that peace would automatically free the full current Ukraine-support budget.

It also does not infer political control or corruption from contacts, networks or economic interests.

## Strategic failure modes

The model would fail if economic concessions strengthened Russian military capacity without durable de-escalation; if European partners did not support the framework; if Ukraine were pressured into an insecure settlement; if Germany recreated a critical energy dependence; if cheaper energy were overestimated while structural competitiveness problems remained unresolved; or if a ceasefire merely created time for renewed military escalation.

Verification, reversibility, diversification and credible deterrence are therefore prerequisites rather than optional additions.

## Falsifiable core hypothesis

> **Can a European strategy combining credible defence, active German mediation, verifiable step-by-step de-escalation, conditional economic normalisation and energy diversification reduce security risks while improving Germany's economic resilience — without imposing an insecure settlement on Ukraine or recreating strategic dependence on Russia?**

The purpose of the project is to make this hypothesis easier to test, criticise and improve. It should be revised or rejected where evidence contradicts it.
